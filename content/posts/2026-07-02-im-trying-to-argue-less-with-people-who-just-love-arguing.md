Arguments are useful, but the actual useful part of an argument is twofold:

- The discussion reveals facts, biases and assumptions that are useful to help determine an outcome or work toward a solution.
- A compromise can be reached, an outcome can be reached, or at the very least, an agreement.

But arguing with someone who is just there to argue, with no stake in a better outcome for all, is completely unfruitful, and i'm trying to get better at recognising these arguments and walk away from them.

It becomes unfruitful when:

- The argument becomes an opportunity for one party to flex their ego and subject matter knowledge in a way that's simply self-aggrandising and doesn't point to a better outcome or solution.
- The other party recognises the emotional investment you have in a subject and takes advantage of that.
- The argument deliberately dismisses legitimate concerns because someone has a known bias. A bias does not always mean the biased opinion is *wrong*, but it does mean it should be more critically evaluated and tested.
- The person arguing, when provided with strong evidence, will not change their mind.

When the argument steers into this territory, i'm trying to find a way to walk away, because it benefits nobody.

## Humility.

I've always wondered about the phrase *strong opinions, weakly held*. I thought it was oddly phrased, but it's true. Jeff Gothelf says ["This is the definition of humility"](https://jeffgothelf.com/blog/strong-opinions-loosely-held/) and as a person who suffers from imposter syndrome in all things that I do, I always try (but let's face it, probably don't succeed 100% of the time) to be humble in all things.

The power of Humility is (in my opinion) in it's ability to prevent you from becoming utterly humiliated by following a point of argument so far that you tie your ego to it, and if or when it becomes proven wrong, becomes a point of humiliation that can lead to anger or resentment.

Swallowing your pride and saying "yeah, you're right" is tough, particularly if you're doing so to someone who is insufferably smug or lacking the humility you're trying to show, but it's the right thing to do, and so few people do the right thing because they're so scared of looking wrong.
