Back when I ran [the bakery](https://instagram.com/inbreadcarlisle), I started thinking of other products that I could sell alongside the bread that might turn a profit (bread itself only really makes profit when sold in bulk).

So i experimented for a few weeks, and this is what I came up with, it's hot but not the kind of painful heat you get from say a pickled Jalapeno, more subtle, but then there's a zing from the lime juice, and depth from all the pepper and garlic. I love this stuff, and so did a few of my customers.

## The Recipe

**1000g Honey** - You don't have to use "good" honey here, as a lot of that flavour can get lost in the infusion, but try to find stuff that is at least "real" honey (not sugar syrup).

**100g Garlic** - use peeled cloves and smash them slightly to release their flavour.

**20g Dried Habenero** - I toasted these lightly in a dry frying pan for a few minutes until fragrant before adding them in.

**100g(ml) Lime Juice** - Fresh, reserve half for stirring in at the end.

**20g Szechuan Peppercorns** - Crush them slightly with the back of your knife/a mortar and pestle but don't break them up too much as it'll make filtering the bits out harder

**50g Black Peppercorns** - Same as above, 'bruise' them, but don't crush into powder.

## The method

Add the honey to a thick walled pan and bring to a medium heat to start with, drop your ingredients with about half the lime juice in and stir well.

Stir until all the ingredients are suspended in the honey, then turn the heat down to low (as low as it'll go), and infuse for about an hour.

Keep coming back and stirring - keep an eye on the garlic, if the heat is too high, the garlic will start taking on colour, and if it burns, will leave the honey with a bitter aftertaste (learned that the hard way).

Once infused, bring the heat up a little more to liquify the honey a little more again, then strain out all the ingredients.

Finally, stir in the last of the lime juice, mix it well.

Leave it to cool before transferring to a jar/container. Use glass if you can - metal will react with the acidity, and plastic will likely discolour. 

## Notes/Substitutions

- Use fresh lime juice, not from a bottle, the acidity is too much, if you're using bottled stuff, mix with about 20% water.
- Peel your garlic, obviously, the skin might impart flavours you don't want.
- You can leave out the szechuan peppercorns if you want or don't have them, but sub with more black peppercorns if you can to punch up the "other" type of heat the honey delivers.
